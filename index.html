Health, Demolition, and Displacement in the West Bank

This visualisation draws on data collected by the UN OCHA in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt). The data tracks the demolition of Palestinian-owned structures, and the resulting displacement of people from their homes, across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, since 2009.

Alongside the demolitions themselves, the dataset records the needs of affected community members across health, livelihood, shelter and water, among others. That makes it possible to explore how these incidents relate to health in a broader sense than physical health alone: one that takes in the link between adverse health outcomes and a severed connection to place. The approach is inspired by Danya M. Qato's 2020 article Public Health and the Promise of Palestine.

Data scraped from ochaopt.org/data/demolition. Click anything to filter everything.

How to read this

Each map ring is a place: its size is the loss and its filled centre is the share of assessed incidents that flagged the need. Brown always means need. The charts below ask how place, event, actor, what was lost and who was affected relate to the body (health care, water), the mind (psychosocial support) and the land (livelihood). Some charts follow your filters; the model charts use all data. Needs are what responders assessed, not clinical outcomes, and are recorded only from 2014.

    How the model works, and what it cannot tell you

    Each need is modelled separately, using incidents assessed since 2014. The model weighs together the reason, who carried it out, incident type, what was lost, displacement, refugee status, place, size of the event, repetition and year. An odds ratio of 2 means the odds of a flag are twice as high.

    What it cannot tell you. These are needs responders recorded, not clinical outcomes, and they follow what was destroyed.

    Source
    Incident records from the OCHA oPt demolition dataset. Data scraped from ochaopt.org/data/demolition. One row is one incident; places are positioned at the median recorded coordinate for the locality. This page reads incidents.csv directly in your browser.

    Needs
    Flags are what responders assessed as needed after an incident. They are first recorded systematically in 2014, so earlier years are drawn but left out of every rate. The free-text health-need field is empty in this dataset and the health flag is rare, so psychosocial support, water and sanitation, and livelihood carry most of the picture. Notes are coded into themes by keyword, which is approximate.

    Map and statistics
    There is no basemap; contours and dotted outlines are drawn from the recorded locations and are not official boundaries. The map, triangle, web, funnel and words charts follow your filters. The driver and strata charts come from one model fitted on all assessed incidents. Association is not causation.
